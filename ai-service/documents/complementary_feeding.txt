COMPLEMENTARY FEEDING FOR CHILDREN 6-24 MONTHS
Practical Guidelines for Mauritanian Families
Source: WHO/UNICEF/Ministry of Health Mauritania

INTRODUCTION
At 6 months, breast milk alone is no longer sufficient to meet all nutritional needs. Complementary feeding (CF) means introducing nutritious, age-appropriate foods while continuing to breastfeed. Poor CF practices are a leading cause of malnutrition, stunting, and child mortality in Mauritania and the Sahel region.

THE FIVE KEY PRINCIPLES OF COMPLEMENTARY FEEDING (IYCF)

1. TIMELY: Start at 6 months — not at 4 months (gut not ready), not at 8 months (nutritional deficit)
2. ADEQUATE: Enough energy, protein, and micronutrients from diverse foods
3. SAFE: Prepared and stored hygienically, fed from clean cups and bowls
4. RESPONSIVE: Feed in response to hunger/fullness cues, encourage but do not force
5. CONTINUED BREASTFEEDING: Breast milk remains important throughout the second year

RECOMMENDED FOODS BY AGE

6-8 MONTHS (Initiation Phase)
Start with smooth, thick porridges. Introduce one new food at a time, waiting 3-5 days to check for reactions.
Recommended foods:
- Thick millet or sorghum porridge (staple in Mauritania) — add oil/butter for energy
- Mashed sweet potato, pumpkin, or carrot (vitamin A)
- Mashed ripe banana or mango
- Soft-cooked lentils, mashed
- Pureed liver (excellent iron and vitamin A)
- Eggs (well-cooked, whole egg including yolk)
Avoid: honey, salt, sugar, whole cow's milk as main drink, hard/chunky foods

9-11 MONTHS (Expansion Phase)
Progress to mashed and finely chopped family foods. Introduce lumpy textures.
Recommended foods:
- All foods from 6-8 months, less pureed
- Mashed cooked fish (remove all bones)
- Ground meat or very soft chicken pieces
- Beans and lentils (cooked soft)
- Soft rice, couscous, or pasta
- Yogurt and fermented dairy
- Moringa leaf powder in porridges
- Varied vegetables and fruits
Introduce finger foods: soft cooked vegetable pieces, banana slices

12-24 MONTHS (Family Foods Phase)
Child can eat most family foods, finely chopped or minced.
Recommended foods:
- All family foods (with less salt/spice)
- Ensure nutrient-dense foods at every meal
- Thieboudienne (fish and rice) is an excellent traditional meal
- Maffe (peanut stew with meat/fish and vegetables)
- Lakh (millet porridge with milk and baobab fruit)
- Thiakry (millet couscous with soured milk)
Continue breastfeeding as part of diet

MEAL FREQUENCY AND AMOUNT
Age 6-8 months: 2-3 tablespoons, 2-3 times/day
Age 9-11 months: 1/2 cup (125ml), 3-4 times/day + 1-2 snacks
Age 12-24 months: 3/4 to 1 full cup (175-250ml), 3-4 times/day + 1-2 snacks
Always offer breastfeeding first, then complementary food at this age

NUTRIENT-DENSE COMBINATIONS TO MAKE AT HOME
Energy boosters: add 1-2 teaspoons of oil/groundnut oil to any porridge
Protein addition: add egg yolk, mashed legumes, ground meat, or dried fish powder
Iron-rich: include liver 1-2x per week; serve with orange juice or mango for absorption
Vitamin A: orange/yellow vegetables daily; moringa leaf powder

MICRONUTRIENT GAPS IN MAURITANIAN CHILDREN
Iron: Most critical gap. Anemia affects 75% of children 6-59 months in Mauritania.
  → Liver, meat, fish, legumes with vitamin C
Vitamin A: Deficiency causes night blindness and increased mortality.
  → Yellow/orange vegetables, eggs, liver, moringa
Zinc: Deficiency impairs immune function and growth.
  → Meat, fish, legumes, whole grains
Calcium: For bone development.
  → Milk, yogurt, moringa leaves, dried small fish

FOODS TO AVOID BEFORE 12 MONTHS
- Salt and salty foods (kidneys not mature)
- Sugar and sweet drinks (no nutritional value, causes dental caries)
- Honey (risk of botulism)
- Cow's milk as main drink (low iron, causes blood loss)
- Whole nuts and large chunks (choking hazard)
- Caffeinated beverages (tea, coffee)
- Processed/packaged foods, chips, biscuits

HYGIENE IN FOOD PREPARATION
- Wash hands before preparing food and before feeding
- Use clean cooking utensils and feeding bowls
- Prepare fresh food — do not store cooked complementary food at room temperature more than 2 hours
- Cover food to prevent flies
- Use clean water for cooking

WARNING SIGNS THAT FEEDING IS INADEQUATE
- Child not gaining weight
- MUAC decreasing (use NurAI app to track)
- Child very irritable, crying frequently
- Child sleepy and weak, not interested in play
- Frequent diarrhea or infections
→ If you notice these signs, visit your health center immediately

SUPPORTING POSITIVE FEEDING PRACTICES
- Feed your child in a calm, positive environment
- Make eye contact and talk to child during feeding
- Never force-feed — respect child's fullness cues
- Offer again if child refuses — sometimes takes 10-15 tries for new food
- Allow child to self-feed when ready (12+ months)
- Sit with child during meals — do not leave unattended
