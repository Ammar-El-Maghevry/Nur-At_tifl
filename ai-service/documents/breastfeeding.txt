BREASTFEEDING GUIDELINES FOR MAURITANIAN MOTHERS
WHO/UNICEF Guidance on Optimal Breastfeeding Practices

THE GLOBAL RECOMMENDATION
The World Health Organization recommends exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months of life, followed by continued breastfeeding with appropriate complementary foods until 2 years of age or beyond.

IMPORTANCE OF EARLY INITIATION
Breastfeeding should begin within 1 hour of birth. The first milk (colostrum) is thick and yellowish — it is often called "liquid gold" because it:
- Is packed with antibodies and immune factors
- Contains growth factors for gut development
- Has a laxative effect that clears meconium
- Prevents neonatal jaundice
- Contains 4x more protein than mature milk

NEVER discard colostrum. Some communities believe it is harmful — this is NOT true. Colostrum is the most valuable nutrition a newborn can receive.

WHAT EXCLUSIVE BREASTFEEDING MEANS
Exclusive breastfeeding means giving ONLY breast milk — no water, no juices, no herbal teas, no other milk (including formula, cow's, goat's), no solid foods. Even in hot climates, breast milk provides all the water a baby needs in the first 6 months.

HOW OFTEN TO BREASTFEED
- Feed on demand — whenever the baby shows hunger cues (rooting, sucking hands, crying)
- Typically 8-12 times per 24 hours in the first months
- Do not follow strict time schedules — respond to baby's needs
- Let baby finish one breast before offering the other
- Night feeding is important — it maintains milk supply

HOW TO KNOW YOUR BABY IS GETTING ENOUGH MILK
Signs of adequate intake:
- Baby feeds 8-12 times per day
- 6 or more wet diapers per day
- Regular soft stools
- Baby seems satisfied after feeds
- Steady weight gain (500-1000g per month in first 3 months)
- Baby is alert and active when awake

BREASTFEEDING POSITIONS
Any comfortable position works. Key principles:
- Baby's body should face mother's body (tummy to tummy)
- Baby's head, neck, and body should be aligned (not twisted)
- Baby's mouth should be WIDE open with chin touching breast
- More areola visible above baby's upper lip than below
- No nipple pain during feeding (pain = incorrect latch)

COMMON BREASTFEEDING PROBLEMS AND SOLUTIONS

Sore nipples:
- Usually caused by poor latch — reposition baby
- Apply breast milk to nipples after feeding and let air dry
- Avoid soap on nipples

Low milk supply:
- Nurse more frequently — supply increases with demand
- Ensure baby has correct latch
- Drink plenty of water and eat nutritious foods
- Avoid giving formula or water (reduces breast stimulation)
- Rest as much as possible
- Skin-to-skin contact increases prolactin

Engorgement:
- Feed baby frequently
- Apply warm compress before feeding
- Cold compress after feeding
- Express some milk if baby cannot latch

BREASTFEEDING AND MATERNAL NUTRITION
Breastfeeding mothers need approximately 500 extra calories per day. Eat:
- Varied diet including cereals, legumes, vegetables, fruits, some animal products
- Plenty of fluids (water, milk, soup)
- Iron-rich foods to recover from birth blood loss
- Continue taking iron and folic acid supplements if available

WHEN NOT TO BREASTFEED (RARE SITUATIONS)
- Mother is HIV-positive AND antiretroviral therapy is not available (if on ART, breastfeeding recommended)
- Infant has galactosemia (extremely rare metabolic disorder)
- Mother is taking certain medications — consult health worker
- Active tuberculosis (untreated) — separate mother until treated

BREASTFEEDING AND CULTURAL PRACTICES IN MAURITANIA
In Mauritanian culture, breastfeeding is traditional and widely practiced. However, some practices may reduce its effectiveness:
- Giving "layt" (sugar water) to newborns — AVOID, give colostrum instead
- Early introduction of fermented milk products — delay until 6 months
- Stopping breastfeeding during maternal illness — continue unless advised otherwise
- Giving herbal teas for "colic" — breast milk is sufficient

WORKING MOTHERS AND BREASTFEEDING
Mothers who return to work can continue breastfeeding:
- Express breast milk manually or with a pump
- Store expressed milk in clean covered container at room temperature for 6-8 hours
- In refrigerator for up to 5 days
- Feed expressed milk by cup (not bottle) to avoid nipple confusion
